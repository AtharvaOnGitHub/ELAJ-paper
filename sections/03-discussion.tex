\section*{Discussion}

\note{PARAGRAPH 1 — what the work establishes, in one move. Not a
summary: the claim at its proper altitude. A sixteen-dimensional
representation of pooled endometrial single-cell data that is blind to
the donor and still holds the menstrual cycle.
\\\textcolor{elajcoral}{CORRECTED 30 October 2026. This note used to end
``and the cycle structure was recovered rather than supplied'', which
a1\_nophase disproves as a bare claim: removing the phase head drops
cross-donor phase from 0.7346 to 0.4812, both already
chance-corrected, so the architecture supplies about 65.5\% of the
signal and the head the remaining 34.5\%. The defensible claim is narrower and better — the
head is one linear map over the whole latent and was never told about
cell types, yet cycle displacement splits by cell type (stromal 1.07,
epithelial 0.98, endothelial 0.39, immune 0.34). THAT structure cannot
have come from the supervision. Claim the split, not the signal.}}

\note{PARAGRAPH 2 — why the frontier framing matters beyond this
dataset. Any single-biopsy design makes the biology collinear with the
sample. Name the other settings where that is true, so the contribution
is not read as endometriosis-specific tooling.}

\note{PARAGRAPH 3 — LIMITATIONS, stated plainly and without hedging
language. Sixteen dimensions against baselines at 128 and higher, so the
comparison is not width-matched. The cycle-day continuity rests on one
cohort. The immune disease result does not replicate across cohorts.
17{,}873 held-out cells (4.3\%) share a donor with training through the
one overlapping study pair. Cell-type labels exist only for endometrium.
\\ADD, AW's ruling 30 October 2026: one study of the eleven (li et al.,
45{,}312 cells, 2.87\% of the corpus, 6 of 269 donors) is a single-cell
atlas of pre-ovulatory follicular fluid collected at IVF oocyte retrieval,
not an endometrial biopsy. It entered the corpus labelled as endometrium and
the model was conditioned accordingly, so the label is reported as it was
given rather than silently corrected. Three consequences to state: the
sample is a fluid aspirate where every other study is a tissue section; its
\texttt{eutopic}/\texttt{control} marks follicular fluid from an ovarian
endometriosis patient against a male-factor infertility control, which is
not what those words mean in the other nine studies, so 2.87\% of the
disease axis carries different semantics; and its cycles are
hormonally stimulated. It contributes NO cell-family and NO phase labels, so
the cycle and cell-type results are untouched by it.}

\note{PARAGRAPH 4 — what this makes possible next, kept concrete.
Avoid the reflexive gesture at clinical translation; say what a sixteen-
number cell representation is actually useful for.}

This work set out to read a pooled endometriosis corpus without losing the
biology that pooling threatens. Eleven studies and 1{,}577{,}728 cells
reduce to sixteen numbers per cell in which the menstrual cycle survives 
but the donor is hard to recover, and that combination is the
point. Every donor here was biopsied once, so menstrual phase is constant
within a donor and varies between donors, which is the geometry of a batch
effect. A correction aimed at the one necessarily reaches for the other.
What survives is more specific than the cycle itself. The phase head
accounts for 34.5\% of the chance-corrected phase margin. The head is a single
linear map over all sixteen dimensions and was never told what kind of cell
it was reading, yet cycle structure in the latent is sharp in stromal
cells, present in epithelial and close to absent in endothelial and immune.
That is the order in which those compartments remodel across the cycle, and
no supervision in this model could have produced it.

The confound is not peculiar to endometriosis. Wherever tissue is hard to
obtain, each donor contributes one sample at one moment, and whatever
varies with that moment becomes collinear with the donor. The field's
usual response is correction strength, turned up until batch
structure disappears. The \elaj\ frontier shows that one number cannot
describe the operation. The adversarial weight moves the achievable
trade-off and stops improving it past a critical point, while the
information cost slides a model along the trade-off without moving it.
Reporting a single correction strength conflates the two. This study offers
a way of working where data is scarce and batch structure is severe: 
state the trade-off, measure both axes, and report the point on the 
frontier the model occupies. Notably, the arm with the best held-out 
reconstruction in this project (\texttt{val\_nb} 0.3911) falls short of the 
reported arm (\texttt{val\_nb} 0.4024) on biology. So validation 
reconstruction loss is a criterion for epoch selection, not for model 
performance.

Size did not produce any of this. \elaj\ carries 1{,}883{,}986
parameters and 16 latent dimensions against pretrained models at 256
to 3{,}072 latent dimensions. Across the ten representations width is 
negatively associated with cross-donor phase agreement ($\rho=-0.6503$, 
$p=0.0418$), and a 16-component PCA reaches 0.6457 on that axis 
against scGPT's 0.4869 at 512 dimensions. For diseases where 
data is scarce and unconsolidated, an efficient model architecture can 
transform what the field is able to say about the disease.

The way the field scores batch correction does not work on a corpus of this
nature. Two of the standard scIB metrics are perfectly degenerate across all
ten representations (iLISI=0.0 and cLISI=1.0 for all, kBET $\leq$ 0.04), one from each half of the scorecard.
The axis used in their place is little better as a ranking instrument:
cross-donor agreement on cell family separates nine of the ten methods by
0.0076 in total (0.9820 to 0.9896), with only Geneformer outside the
band (0.9023). Cell identity is something every method here recovers and
therefore something that cannot rank methods. Agreement on disease status 
tracks donor recovery across the ten ($\rho=+0.721$, $p=0.019$) while 
agreement on menstrual phase does not ($+0.176$). Disease status is 
constant within a donor, so a representation that remembers donors is 
handed the disease label at no cost, and Methods quantifies how large that 
free component is. A disease score reported on pooled single-cell data is 
therefore partly a memorisation score, and the methods that look strongest 
on it are the ones from which donors are most easily recovered.

Revised American Society for Reproductive Medicine (rASRM) scoring rates 
what a surgeon sees. The size and depth of lesions and the adhesions between 
organs determine staging in endometriosis. It was built to predict fertility 
outcomes rather than symptoms. Read through the latent, the scale comes apart.
Whether a person has endometriosis is recoverable from their endometrium, and
recoverable even in the representation from which donors are hardest to
identify (0.3364, $p=0.005$). Severity is legible only because it carries
a contrast between disease and no disease; among donors with disease alone the
grades do not separate ($p=0.129$), and nothing tested here does better, 
including four models pretrained on corpora orders of magnitude larger than
this corpus. However, the presence of disease is legible in endometrium sampled 
from its normal location while the rASRM stage is not. It is
nonetheless what every cohort records, what trials stratify on, and the
number a patient is handed to account for their pain. It has never tracked
how much a person hurts, and patients have been saying so for decades.
The staging worth building is a readout from tissue reachable in
a clinic rather than anatomical cues. Diagnosis today takes 11.1 years and 
ends in laparoscopy \citep{bontempo2025diagnostic}. The case for changing both 
the scale and the route rests on every patient told that a lesion a 
millimetre across could not account for their pain.

Several limits bound these conclusions. Width is not matched: \elaj\ is read
at sixteen dimensions against representations at 256 to 3{,}072, although
the negative association between width and cycle agreement makes it unlikely
that width is what separates them. The continuity of cycle day rests on a
single cohort of 60 donors, so the closed trajectory is replicated nowhere
else in this corpus. One of the eleven studies, 45{,}312 cells and 2.87\% of
the corpus from 6 donors, is a follicular-fluid aspirate that entered
labelled as endometrium and was conditioned accordingly; it contributes no
cell-family and no phase labels, so the cycle and cell-type results do not
depend on it. The corpus also holds only a part of the metadata that
matters, since no cohort records cycle day, serum progesterone, surgical
stage and treatment history together. Finally, the permutation null
reassigns the donor-to-label map across all eleven studies at once, which
breaks the association between a label and the cohort it came from, so the
null never sees whatever study structure remains in the latent. The size of
that residue is our own study separation figure, 0.7613, and a permutation
stratified within study is the correct remedy.

What a sixteen-number representation of a cell is useful for is narrow and
worth stating plainly. It gives a common coordinate system in which a new
cohort can be placed beside eleven existing ones without being corrected
into them, which is the operation every one of the questions this field
cannot currently answer depends on. It makes a cell's position comparable
across studies, so a pattern reported in one can be looked for in another
rather than argued about. And it is small enough to retrain, so the next
cohort to be published does not require anyone to repeat a pretraining run
in order to be included.
