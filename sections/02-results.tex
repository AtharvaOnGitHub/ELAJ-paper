\section*{Results}

The results of this study are divided across 2 main categories. The first is 
about how \elaj performs against foundation models and other baselines. These 
represent a numerical evaluation of the model, describe the frontier, and 
outline model performance against benchmarks. The second category of results is 
understanding what \elaj itself is doing. This covers analysis of the \elaj 
latent space, ablation studies, and other ways of understanding the model itself.

\subsection*{[1] The \elaj frontier}

Six models were trained, varying the adversarial weight over three values at
two settings of the latent's information cost. The best model was chosen on 
reconstruction loss value on the validation set. (Fig.~1b, c).

At the lower information cost, raising the adversarial weight from the lowest
to the middle value reduced donor recovery from 0.5837 to 0.5188 and study
mixing from 0.7183 to 0.6043, while cross-donor phase agreement was 0.7346
against 0.7337 and cell family 0.9820 against 0.9829. Raising it to the
highest value gave donor recovery 0.5277, study mixing 0.6530 and phase
0.7146.

Raising the information cost reduced donor recovery and study mixing at every
adversarial weight, to a minimum of 0.3975 and 0.5129, and reduced
cross-donor phase agreement to between 0.5922 and 0.6791 and cell family to
between 0.9553 and 0.9841.

All results below are reported from the model at the middle adversarial
weight and the lower information cost: cross-donor phase agreement 0.7346
against a chance rate of 0.370, cell family 0.9820, donor recovery 0.5188 and
study mixing 0.6043.

\subsection*{[3] The menstrual cycle through \elaj}

The decoder is conditioned on study, platform and tissue alone. Menstrual phase 
was the only supervised label, provided to the latent through a single linear 
map over all sixteen dimensions. Other related metadata variables such as cycle 
day, serum progesterone, cell type and disease status were never provided as 
inputs to the model.

Cycle day, recorded for 466{,}176 cells in only one cohort of 60 donors 
representing 22 days between day 5 and 30, showed up in the latent space. 
Advancing through the days one at a time showed a dense mass of cells being 
progressively arranged in the latent space. As the cycle returned to where it 
began, so did that region, showing the cycle to be closed in the latent space 
(Fig.~5a).

Cross-donor agreement, defined in Methods, is the share of a cell's hundred
nearest latent neighbours that carry its label, counting only the neighbours
belonging to other donors, and rescaled so that zero is the agreement the
label's frequencies alone would produce and one is perfect. Significance is
assessed by reassigning the same labels to different donors two hundred times
and recomputing \cite{newman2003mixing,anderson2001permanova}, the donor being
the unit that can be exchanged when a label is constant within one
\cite{squair2021confronting}. On held-out donors, agreement on menstrual phase
was 0.7346 across 36 donors ($n_{\text{cells}}=195{,}879$), and no permutation
reached it ($p=0.005$, the smallest value two hundred permutations can return).
It remained high when all 1{,}577{,}728 cells were read together, at 0.6796
across 142 donors ($p=0.005$). 

Removing the phase head reduced that agreement to 0.4812, so just under
two-thirds of the signal survived the loss of supervision (65.5\%) and the
head accounted for the remaining third.

Epithelial pseudotime and serum progesterone correlated highly (0.58 and 0.47, 
respectively) with the single best-matched of the sixteen
dimensions of the \elaj latent space. This was 
calibrated against four measures with a range of correlation between 0.14 and 
0.34 with the highest being the fraction of mitochondrial reads.

\subsection*{[4] Disease signal, and where it is not}

Disease status reached significance only when cohorts were pooled. Across the
pooled held-out cells, cross-donor agreement on it was 0.3364
($n_{\text{cells}}=172{,}740$; $p=0.005$). Measured inside each cohort
separately, which keeps each study's donors with their own labels, it was
0.1350 in marecko\_2024 over sixteen labelled donors ($p=0.3433$) and 0.0636
in duempelmann\_2026 over eighteen ($p=0.0697$); lin\_2023 contributes two
labelled donors, for which the smallest attainable $p$ is 1.000 and the test
returns nothing. Read across all 1{,}577{,}728 cells, agreement on disease
severity in each study's own vocabulary reached 0.2655 over 118 donors and 67
label levels ($p=0.005$), the largest donor count any disease label in this
corpus supports.

Surgical staging was not recoverable on held-out donors. Stage is recorded in
one cohort only, and sixteen of its donors fall in the held-out split; their
agreement was 0.1313, lower than reassigning the same stages to different
donors produces, and not significant ($p=0.3035$). Across the whole corpus,
where the same label spans 58 donors and includes those the model was trained
on, it reached 0.332 and was significant ($p=0.005$). Grade is recorded in a
different single cohort, with eighteen donors held out; agreement there was
negative ($-$0.0414; $p=0.4975$) and near zero across the corpus over 60
donors (0.0263; $p=0.408$). It remained negative in all six ablated
configurations, between $-$0.0227 and $-$0.0723. Cell family and menstrual
phase, measured the same way on the same cells, both reach the permutation
floor of 0.005 (Fig.~4d).

Where disease signal did appear, it appeared in one compartment of one
cohort. Within marecko\_2024 the immune cells separated case from control
more than any other cell family did, and the same compartment led again in
the arm trained at the higher information cost (Fig.~4b). The pattern did not
carry to duempelmann\_2026, where the separation was roughly a third as large
and the leading compartment was not even the same in the two arms
(Fig.~4c).
