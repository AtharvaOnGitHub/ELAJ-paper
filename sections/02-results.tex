\section*{Results}

The results of this study are divided across 2 main categories. The first is 
about how \elaj performs against foundation models and other baselines. These 
represent a numerical evaluation of the model, describe the frontier, and 
outline model performance against benchmarks. The second category of results is 
understanding what \elaj itself is doing. This covers analysis of the \elaj 
latent space, ablation studies, and other ways of understanding the model itself.

% ---------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection*{[1] The \elaj frontier}

Six models were trained, varying the adversarial weight over three values at
two settings of the latent's information cost. The best model was chosen on 
reconstruction loss value on the validation set. (Fig.~1b, c).

At the lower information cost, raising the adversarial weight from the lowest
to the middle value reduced donor recovery from 0.5837 to 0.5188 and study
mixing from 0.7183 to 0.6043, while cross-donor phase agreement was 0.7346
against 0.7337 and cell family 0.9820 against 0.9829. Raising it to the
highest value gave donor recovery 0.5277, study mixing 0.6530 and phase
0.7146.

Raising the information cost reduced donor recovery and study mixing at every
adversarial weight, to a minimum of 0.3975 and 0.5129, and reduced
cross-donor phase agreement to between 0.5922 and 0.6791 and cell family to
between 0.9553 and 0.9841.

All results below are reported from the model at the middle adversarial
weight and the lower information cost: cross-donor phase agreement 0.7346
against a chance rate of 0.370, cell family 0.9820, donor recovery 0.5188 and
study mixing 0.6043.

\subsection*{[3] The cycle is a cycle, and it moves only the cells it should}

The decoder is conditioned on study, platform and tissue alone. Cycle day,
serum progesterone, cell type and disease status were never inputs, and
menstrual phase was the only supervised label, reaching the latent through a
single linear map over all sixteen dimensions.

Cross-donor agreement on menstrual phase was high on held-out donors, at twice
the chance rate (0.7346 against 0.370; $n=195{,}879$; $p=0.005$, the
permutation floor), and remained high when all 1{,}577{,}728 cells were
embedded and read together (0.6796 across 142 donors; $p=0.005$).

The cycle did not displace every cell type equally. Measured between the
proliferative and secretory centroids in units of each cell type's own
scatter, stromal and epithelial cells moved by about a full width (1.07 and
0.98) while endothelial and immune cells moved by roughly a third of one (0.39
and 0.34) (Fig.~2a). The arm trained at the higher information cost reproduced
both the ordering and the gap between the two pairs (1.07, 0.99, 0.54 and
0.29), though the two smaller values themselves were not stable between arms
(Fig.~2b).

Supervision supplied most of that phase signal: removing the phase head
reduced cross-donor agreement to 0.4812, leaving 30.5\% of the margin over
chance.

Two continuous quantities that were never model inputs tracked the latent more
closely than any quality-control measure did, by a real but modest margin.
Epithelial pseudotime and serum progesterone correlated at 0.58 and 0.47 with
the best-correlated of the sixteen dimensions, against 0.34 for mitochondrial
fraction, 0.34 for genes detected, 0.25 for total counts and 0.14 for doublet
score under the identical procedure.

Cycle day was recorded for under a third of the corpus (466{,}176 cells,
29.6\%), in one cohort of 60 donors who each contributed a single day between
day 5 and day 30, 22 of those 26 days being represented. Advancing through
those days one at a time moved the dense region of the embedding progressively
and returned it to where it began (Fig.~2c--e).

Donor identity nonetheless remained strongly local: a third of a cell's
hundred nearest neighbours came from its own donor (0.327 against a chance
rate of 0.006, a 54-fold enrichment), which is why every agreement figure
above discards same-donor neighbours.

\subsection*{[4] Disease signal, and where it is not}

Within marecko\_2024, case and control cells were separated most in the immune
compartment, where the displacement between their centroids reached
three-quarters of that compartment's own width (0.77), ahead of epithelial,
endothelial and stromal cells (0.61, 0.58 and 0.43) (Fig.~4a). The immune
compartment led again, and by more, in the arm at the higher information cost
(0.83). For scale, whole-corpus separations between cell families measured
identically span 0.76 for epithelial against stromal cells to 1.53 for
endothelial against immune.

The pattern did not carry to the second cohort. In duempelmann\_2026 every
family fell to roughly a quarter of a width (0.19--0.29 across both arms), and
the leading compartment was not the same in the two arms --- immune in one,
endothelial in the other (Fig.~4b).

Cross-donor neighbour agreement on disease status, a different measurement
that discards same-donor neighbours, was modest pooled across studies (0.3364;
$n=172{,}740$) and weaker within each cohort taken separately (0.135 in
marecko\_2024, 0.064 in duempelmann\_2026, 0.000 in lin\_2023). Agreement on
disease severity in each study's own vocabulary, read across all 1{,}577{,}728
cells and 118 donors, was comparable (0.2655; $p=0.005$).

Surgical staging was not recoverable on held-out donors. Stage, recorded for 58
donors in a single cohort, reached a cross-donor agreement of $+$0.1313 but
came nowhere near significance ($p=0.3035$); across the whole corpus, which
includes the donors the model was trained on, it was both larger and
significant ($+$0.332; $p=0.005$). Grade, recorded for 60 donors in a
different single cohort, was negative on held-out donors ($-$0.0414;
$p=0.4975$) and near zero across the corpus ($+$0.0263; $p=0.408$), and
remained negative in all six ablated configurations ($-$0.0227 to $-$0.0723;
$p$ between 0.4378 and 0.6269). Cell family and menstrual phase, by contrast,
both reach the permutation floor of 0.005 (Fig.~4c).
