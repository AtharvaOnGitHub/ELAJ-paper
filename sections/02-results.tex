\subsection*{[1] The \elaj frontier}


Six models were trained, varying the adversarial weight over three values at
each of two settings of the latent's information cost. Predictions for each
were registered in its configuration file before training began. All six were
scored on held-out donors for cross-donor agreement on menstrual phase and
cell family, and for donor and study recovery (Fig.~1b, c).

At the lower information cost, raising the adversarial weight from the lowest
to the middle value reduced donor recovery from 0.5837 to 0.5188 and study
mixing from 0.7183 to 0.6043, while cross-donor phase agreement was 0.7346
against 0.7337 and cell family 0.9820 against 0.9829. Raising it to the
highest value gave donor recovery 0.5277, study mixing 0.6530 and phase
0.7146.

Raising the information cost reduced donor recovery and study mixing at every
adversarial weight, to a minimum of 0.3975 and 0.5129, and reduced
cross-donor phase agreement to between 0.5922 and 0.6791 and cell family to
between 0.9553 and 0.9841.

All results below are reported from the model at the middle adversarial
weight and the lower information cost: cross-donor phase agreement 0.7346
against a chance rate of 0.370, cell family 0.9820, donor recovery 0.5188 and
study mixing 0.6043.

\subsection*{[2] \elaj\ against the field}


\elaj\ was compared with eleven methods on the same held-out donors: a
sixteen-component principal component decomposition, scVI, scANVI, Harmony,
Scanorama, and four pretrained single-cell foundation models. Methods that
can be fitted inductively were fitted on the training donors and applied to
held-out ones; the rest are reported as transductive. Supervision is declared
per method, and \elaj's is menstrual phase (Fig.~3).

Cross-donor phase agreement was higher for \elaj\ than for any other method
tested, at 0.7346 against 0.7053 for scVI, 0.6971 for scANVI and 0.6906 for
the principal component decomposition; chance was 0.370. The four foundation
models, at between 256 and 3{,}072 dimensions, reached 0.6449, 0.6255, 0.4869
and 0.4372.

Study mixing was 0.4712 for \elaj\ and between 0.6284 and 0.8076 for the
others, and donor recovery was lower for \elaj\ than for any other method
tested.

Cell family agreement was 0.9841 for \elaj, against 0.9896 for SCimilarity
and 0.9835 for scANVI, both of which were supervised on cell type, and 0.9868
for the principal component decomposition, which was not.

Cross-donor disease agreement did not exceed 0.3787 for \elaj. Three
baselines exceeded that figure, to a maximum of 0.4486.

\subsection*{[4] The cycle is a cycle, and it moves only the cells it should}
\note{CARRIES C2, C3. FIGURE 2. THE HEADLINE.
\\C2, AND THE ARGUMENT MATTERS MORE THAN THE NUMBERS: displacement in units
of each cell type's own scatter --- stromal 1.07, epithelial 0.98,
endothelial 0.39, immune 0.34, identical across both frontier arms. The

cell-type structure of the cycle was recovered'' is.
\\C3: day 8 through 15--20 to 25--30 with the ends meeting, read off a
cyclic colour map rather than inferred.
\\HONEST LIMIT IN THE SAME BREATH, not a footnote: the day values come from
one cohort, 60 donors, and every one of them contributes exactly one day.
\\\textcolor{elajcoral}{DO NOT CLAIM donors are spread across the
embedding. They are not. Three donors at days 6, 12 and 29 occupy visibly
different regions, and donor identity is 14$\times$ enriched among a cell's
thirty nearest neighbours against chance. That is the confound showing in
our own latent, and it is the reason the phase number discards same-donor
neighbours. If a panel shows donor colour, it argues for the estimator, not
for the result.}
