**Paragraph 2, rewritten whole** (guide: rephrase everything but the last line; AW's funnel)

*Now:* "Endometriosis follows the menstrual cycle [1,9], in which the endometrium is shed and regenerated within days [16], so its measurement requires a similar fidelity. Metabolomic and proteomic profiles shift with diet, time of day and physical activity within hours [Dallmann 2012; Depner 2018; Contrepois 2020], making daily changes harder to detect. The transcriptome follows both disease and cycle [HECA] and is read genome-wide. Within transcriptomics, bulk sequencing averages expression across cell types [Haque 2017] and spatial methods remain too costly for cohort scale [Moses 2022], leaving single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA) as the one readout with daily fidelity, cellular resolution and enough public data to pool. scRNA studies show that lesions carry transcriptional programmes of their own [3,4], that the endometrium of an affected person is already altered [5,6] and that immune involvement recurs [7,8], against a reference of a healthy cycle [9]. Single-cell transcriptomics has thus shown promise in groups of tens of donors [5,9] and has established methods for collating them."

**New paragraph after paragraph 2** (guide: what models were used before foundation models)

"Collating scRNA studies requires correcting batch effects, the differences in sample handling, sequencing platform and other non-biological factors between studies [Luecken 2022]. Harmony iteratively clusters cells in principal-component space to mix batches [Korsunsky 2019], and Scanorama stitches datasets together through mutual nearest neighbours [Hie 2019]. scVI instead models raw counts with a variational autoencoder conditioned on batch [Lopez 2018], and scANVI extends it with cell-type labels where they exist [Xu 2021]. A benchmark of 68 method and preprocessing combinations found, however, that preprocessing alone can push a method to remove batch at the expense of biology [Luecken 2022]. Removing batch effects without removing biology therefore remains an open problem."

**Paragraph 4, last sentence** (guide: rephrase)

*Was:* "However, the field's own review of atlas building reports no recipe for doing this: there is "no one-size-fits-all protocol for atlas building" [20]."

*Now:* "While the individual results from these studies are promising, a recent review of atlas construction concludes that no single protocol applies to all disease atlases and thus a tailored approach based on the pathophysiology of the disease is needed [20]."

**Paragraphs 5 and 6 merged into one** (guide: combine, start "In this study", explain what we do). Removes the withdrawn claim "discover new genes associated with endometriosis".

*Now:* "In this study, we collate every publicly available single-cell study of endometriosis into a corpus of 1,577,728 cells from 254 donors across eleven studies. ELAJ is a single-cell disease atlas of endometriosis built on that corpus as a 1,883,986-parameter transformer-based conditional variational autoencoder (CVAE) [21,22] that reads a cell's genes in 44 pathway groups and compresses each cell into a sixteen-dimensional interpretable latent space. ELAJ replaces foundation-model scale with inductive biases adapted to the disease (Fig. 1a,b). Because every donor in the corpus was biopsied once, menstrual phase is constant within a donor and indistinguishable from a batch effect, a case the field's guidance on batch covariates does not cover [20], making it difficult to maximise batch removal and biological signal together. ELAJ traces a Pareto front between donor-blindness and retained cycle signal, which we call the ELAJ frontier because it outperforms all nine competing representations, four of them foundation models. Every representation is scored by cross-donor agreement, a measure we define to evaluate disease atlases on labels recovered only from other donors' cells. With this atlas we show that biology withheld from the model emerges from training, that surgical staging of endometriosis is not separable in any of ten representations, and that the latent separates patients from controls while its per-donor disease axes trace to cell composition, the menstrual cycle and the sample. We also use the atlas to test claims made by smaller datasets at a much larger scale. Ablation studies validate the architectural assumptions behind these results."
