# Methods

**4.** Section *Pathway gene grouping*. After the paragraph ending "and no
data-losing assumptions have been made.", insert these three paragraphs:

> Gene membership was taken from the Reactome 2022 and GO Biological Process
> 2023 libraries, and each hierarchy from its own relation file, restricted
> to human pathways for Reactome. Both hierarchies were cut with the same
> procedure, which holds every group between 250 and 500 genes
> (Supplementary Note 6). Starting from the roots, any node with more than
> 500 genes was replaced by its children until no node exceeded that
> ceiling. Genes annotated to a replaced node but to none of its children
> were kept as a residual group of that node. Each gene was then assigned to
> the smallest node containing it, since both hierarchies allow a gene
> several parents, with ties broken by node identifier. Finally, groups
> below 250 genes were merged, smallest first, into the partner sharing
> their most specific common ancestor, preferring the smaller of two equally
> related partners. Each merged group is therefore one branch of the
> hierarchy and is named after that ancestor.
>
> No merge was allowed to exceed 500 genes. The ceiling bounds the memory of
> attention, and because groups run in size buckets padded to their largest
> member (*Optimisation*), one oversized group is paid for by every group in
> its bucket. One group of 247 genes is left below the floor as a result.
>
> Reactome was cut first, over all 30,962 genes of the corpus, and assigned
> 10,114 genes to 28 groups. It was given precedence because it is manually
> curated and organised by pathway, whereas GO is broader and structured as
> a graph rather than a tree. GO was then cut over the genes Reactome had
> not assigned, adding 5,382 genes in 16 groups. The resulting vocabulary
> has 15,496 genes in 44 groups of 247 to 493 genes, median 342, with every
> gene in exactly one group (Supplementary Data 5). The grouping was fixed
> before training and never adjusted.

**6.** End of section *Comparison methods*. After the paragraph ending "so
cell family is not a withheld label for it.", insert a new subsection
heading **Label transfer** (same style as *Comparison methods*) and these
three paragraphs:

> Only two studies annotated their own cells. `duempelmann_2026` and
> `marecko_2024` supply a cell family for 640,606 cells from 118 donors. To
> compare cell families across the whole corpus, each of the remaining
> 937,122 cells was given the family held by the majority of its 25 nearest
> annotated cells, by Euclidean distance in the latent of the reported model
> and with ties broken in a fixed order. The nine target studies contribute
> no annotated cells, so every transfer crosses both a donor and a study by
> construction.
>
> The transfer uses the four families the two studies share: stromal,
> epithelial, endothelial and immune. Finer annotation was available in each
> study separately but the labels could not be combined. Each assignment
> records the share of the 25 neighbours that voted for the winning family,
> their mean distance, and how many donors and studies they came from, so
> that any downstream analysis can restrict itself to confident calls.
>
> The transfer was validated in two ways before any label was written. The
> first tests whether a label survives the move from one study to another.
> Each annotated study was used as the reference for 60,000 cells of the
> other, and a balanced accuracy above 0.90 was required. Labels from
> `marecko_2024` recovered the annotation of `duempelmann_2026` at a
> balanced accuracy of 0.9596, and the reverse at 0.9640, with accuracies of
> 0.9862 and 0.9845. The second tests a tissue absent from both references.
> `li_2025` profiles follicular fluid and annotates its cells in its own
> vocabulary, so its immune cells were required to be called immune. They
> were, at an accuracy of 0.9966. Its granulosa cells have no endometrial
> counterpart and were assigned to an existing family without being flagged
> (Supplementary Note 5).
